**What's new**
- **Extended values for newer Subarus (experimental).** In OBD-II mode you can now ask the car for manufacturer specific values such as **AVCS (VVT) angles, knock and boost control**, using OBD-II Mode 22. Turn it on in Settings, connect, and SubieScope checks which of about 130 known values your car answers and offers those in the Logger. Most cars answer none of them (a 2008 WRX STI answered nothing), so this is mainly for Subarus from about 2015. The values come from community data, so please check them against what you expect. Tested with a simulated car only: reports from real cars are very welcome.
- **Pressure in bar.** Boost, manifold and fuel pressures can now be shown in kPa, bar or psi. Pick one in Settings > Pressure and it applies everywhere.
- **Nine more standard values.** The car reports more than the app offered. Added: fuel system status (open or closed loop), the second throttle and pedal sensors, relative pedal position, and the counters for warm-ups, time and distance since codes were cleared and with the check engine light on. Each was checked against a real car.
- **Command line remote control (for developers).** `subiescope-cli remote` sends read-only requests to the adapter the running app is connected to, so things can be tried against a real car without rebuilding the app. It is off by default, only your own user can reach it, and nothing that clears codes or writes to the car is allowed.
- **Fixed:** a connected Bluetooth adapter showed as "not in range", because adapters stop advertising while connected. Its name is now remembered.

**Install:** download `SubieScope.dmg` below, open it and drag SubieScope to Applications (replace the old version if you have one). You need macOS 14 or newer, and either a VAG KKL cable with an FTDI chip or a Bluetooth 4.0 (BLE) ELM327 adapter.
